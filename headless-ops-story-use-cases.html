The story — Tuesday, 14:03 UTC
What Priya experienced. What the system did. When and why she was pulled in.

It's 14:03 UTC on a Tuesday. Priya is in a product review meeting, laptop closed, phone face-down on the table. She has not looked at a dashboard since Monday morning. She doesn't need to — the system is running headless, and silence is the signal that everything is fine.

At that exact moment, three things are happening simultaneously in the background that she knows nothing about yet. The IBM Network Intelligence agents are observing, correlating, and acting — or in one case, holding and waiting for her.

Priya's total involvement in a P1 incident that spanned 8 hours of system activity: one Slack message read, one approval tapped, 30 seconds of her time. The system handled the rest — investigation, ticket, CR, maintenance window selection, canary deployment, and closure.

The headless contract in action: The system resolved 33 issues silently that week. It pulled Priya in exactly 3 times — each time with a self-contained decision card that took under 30 seconds to act on. She never opened a dashboard proactively. The dashboard existed as a deep-dive surface, not an attention surface.
Three use cases — from the prototype data
1
INC-8841 · P1 · payment-service · us-east-1
DB connection pool exhausted — guardrail blocks restart, human approves alternative
Remediation Agent · Guardrail: G-01 Hard · Decision time: 30 sec · Resolution: 8 hrs end-to-end
Situation
At 14:03 UTC, the connection pool on payment-service peaked at 498 of 500 (99.6%), rising at +12 connections per minute. Root cause: a connection leak in v2.3.9 — a third-party ORM library failing to release connections under load above 400 concurrent requests. The blast radius was limited to payment-service only.
What the system did autonomously
The Remediation Agent detected the anomaly, matched it against 14 similar historical cases (confidence 91%), and identified the preferred fix: pod restart. It then hit guardrail G-01 — "no prod restarts weekdays 14:00–16:00 UTC" — and stopped. It did not restart anyway. It did not silently ignore the guardrail. It generated an alternative recommendation, assembled the evidence bundle, and issued a decision card.

2
INC-8839 · P2 · TVM-EDGE-04 · Hardware
Recurring CRC errors — hardware degradation confirmed, CR pending approval
Fault Detection Agent · Guardrail: none triggered · Confidence: 87% · Stage: CR pending
Situation
TVM-EDGE-04 has produced CRC burst errors on optic port 7/4 four times in seven days, with intermittent signal loss averaging every 3.1 hours. This is not a software issue — the Fault Detection Agent has confirmed hardware degradation. 1,200 subscribers are at risk of signal drop but are currently being served via a restart loop (Sequence A→B→C, 6-minute recovery window each time).
What the system did autonomously
On the first three occurrences, the agent applied the restart sequence each time and restored service autonomously. On the fourth, it detected the hardware degradation pattern (87% confidence), opened an investigation record, auto-created Jira ticket NOC-4419, notified NOC L2 Team, and drafted Change Request CR-2024-0145 with a pre-filled risk rating (Low), rollback plan ("revert to previous optic, ETA <10 min"), and recommended maintenance window. No alert was sent to Priya on the first three occurrences — they were silent auto-resolutions.

3
DEC-003 · Info · api-gateway · ap-southeast-1
Novel routing anomaly — confidence below threshold, system holds and asks
Network Agent · Guardrail: G-03 Confidence · Confidence: 62% · No immediate breach
Situation
An unusual latency spike pattern appeared on api-gateway in ap-southeast-1. P95 latency rose by 140ms — still within SLO, but the pattern shape was unlike any of the 4,820 events in the historical case base. The Network Agent scored its confidence at 62%, which is below the autonomous action threshold of 85% set by guardrail G-03.
Why the system held
Guardrail G-03 exists precisely for this scenario: the system is uncertain and acting on a 62% confidence pattern could make things worse. Rather than guessing, the agent queued an info-level decision card in the inbox with two options: approve a read-only diagnostic run (no changes, 3-minute report), or escalate to a Level-2 NOC engineer. The system did not page anyone, did not act, and did not flood Slack — it waited.
Evidence presented to the operator
Confidence score62% — threshold: 85% (G-03)
Pattern typeNovel — no historical match in 4,820 cases
Current impactP95 latency +140ms (within SLO)
UrgencyLow — no SLO breach imminent
Recommended actionRead-only diagnostic run — no changes, 3-min report
Why human is needed: The system genuinely does not know what is causing this. Guardrail G-03 is the confidence threshold — it is not a freeze window or a cost ceiling, it is a humility boundary. Below 85% certainty on a novel pattern, the system's job is to stop and ask, not to act and potentially escalate a contained anomaly into a real incident.
Design principle shown: The headless model does not mean the system always acts. It means the system acts when confident and asks when not. The decision card for this scenario carries no countdown timer, no red banner — it is informational. Priya can approve the diagnostic at her convenience, or escalate. Either way, she has the full picture in one card.
