CTA buttons		 Move forward
Go back		 Non-standard action labels. Carbon standard for stepped flows is Next (advance) and Back (return). "Move forward" is colloquial and not verb-first. "Go back" adds an unnecessary word. Next / Back
High Panel 0 — Overview
Condition pill		 Likely condition · Designing for Invisible UI Inconsistent label pattern. Other pills use state · topic (e.g. "Pattern named · What & Why"). "Likely condition" reads as a diagnostic qualifier, not a state label. It creates a false impression the content is tentative or provisional. Overview · Designing for Invisible UI
High Footer
Restart button		 Start again Non-standard action label. Carbon standard for restarting a flow is Start over or simply Start. "Start again" is informal and inconsistent with product conventions. Start over
High Panel 2 — Characteristics
Heading		 Four Defining Behaviours UK English spelling. IBM Content Standard requires US English. "Behaviours" → "Behaviors". Same issue exists in body copy throughout. Four Defining Behaviors
High All panels
Body copy (multiple)		 behaviour / behaviours, artefact, authorise UK English throughout. IBM standard requires US English. All three spellings appear in the site. artefact → artifact, authorise → authorize. behavior / behaviors, artifact, authorize

Medium Panel 3 — Four Pillars
Agent Identity quote		 "Trust and accountability must surface. This answers the W's — who acted, what was the policy, when did it run, why was permission granted." Ambiguous label "the W's". This is informal jargon and opaque to non-native English readers. IBM voice is direct and factual — name the questions explicitly. "Trust and accountability must be visible — every agent action should be traceable to who acted, what policy applied, when it ran, and why permission was granted."
Medium Panel 4 — Directions
Direction 05 example label		 Example Questions Inconsistent label across 5 cards. Directions 01–04 use Example; Direction 05 uses Example Questions. Use the same label throughout. Example
Medium Panel 4 — Directions
Direction card body text		 These are not rules — they are orientations. Weak framing. Defining by what something is not ("not rules") weakens the opening. IBM voice leads with a positive, confident point of view. Five shifts in how designers think when working with headless AI agents. Click any card to reveal the example.
Medium Top nav
Step sub-labels		 Canvas view, Pattern named, Useful words, Owner + follow-up, Evidence in work Sub-labels are opaque to first-time users. These appear to be internal design-process stage names, not descriptors of what a user will find in each step. Remove, or replace with: Introduction, Definition, Characteristics, Framework, Directions
Medium Panel 3 — Rail
SVG diagram footer label		 HEADLESS AI · FOUR PILLARS · ALWAYS ACTIVE ALL CAPS in body content. IBM Content Standard requires sentence case for all UI text. Headless AI · Four pillars · Always active

02. Passed Patterns & Strong Content Practices

Elements in the current design that successfully adhere to IBM Content and UX standards.

Compliant
Strong Value Proposition (Panel 0): "Autonomous AI agents don't wait for humans to open a window. They act in the background — monitoring telemetry, correlating anomalies, executing remediation scripts. The interface disappears." Direct, active voice, problem-first.
Effective Analogy (Panel 1): "Think of headless AI like an immune system: constantly active, quiet by design, surfacing only when an anomaly requires elevated intervention." Anchors a novel paradigm in a familiar, intuitive mental model.
Concrete Design Directions (Panel 4): Each direction is actionable ("Design for ambient presence, not primary attention"), provides a real-world example, and gives designers practical criteria.
