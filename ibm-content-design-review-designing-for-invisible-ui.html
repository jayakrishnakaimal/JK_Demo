What's working well

IBM voice is strong throughout. The body copy is confident, factual, and point-of-view-led. Phrases like "Design the event payload, not the UI form" and "Chat is not the product — it's an emergency hatch" are direct, purposeful, and intellectually ambitious — hallmarks of Level 3 IBM voice.
Analogy use is effective. The immune system analogy in Panel 1 is clear, grounded, and memorable. IBM voice guidelines endorse figurative language for emphasis — this lands well.
Design principles are action-oriented. The rail mock card principles ("The permission matrix IS the product spec", "Identity is not metadata — it is accountability made visible") are confident and instructional. Retain this pattern.
Descriptions are appropriately technical without being jargon-heavy. The Panel 2 characteristics tabs provide the right depth for a design-team audience. Context, examples, and implications are all present.
Error/alert content in micro-interactions is well-formed. The Scene 02 alert trace rows state what happened, the confidence score, and the required approval — factual and economical.

Coverage gaps

  • MacBook SVG text strings (Hello Fiona, Good morning, chart labels) are decorative/illustrative — not reviewed for content standards.
  • Live agent terminal log lines (streamed JS content) were not reviewed — they are demo/simulation content rather than product UI strings.
  • Hover and focus states, screen-reader accessible names on icon buttons (Pause, Replay, gallery expand) are implementation concerns outside the scope of this content review.
  • The Dismiss × button pattern merges a label with an icon glyph — flagged as medium; ARIA implementation not inspected.
