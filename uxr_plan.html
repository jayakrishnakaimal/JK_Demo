Section 01

1 Background & context

The IBM Network Intelligence team is exploring a new interaction paradigm for AI-powered network operations: headless AI agents that detect anomalies, make remediation decisions, and take actions autonomously — surfacing to human operators only for escalations, approvals, or audit.

The concept introduces three novel surfaces that operators have never encountered before: a Canvas that shows ambient system confidence rather than alerts, an Agentic Trace that exposes agent decision reasoning, and a Policy & Permissions studio where operators define what an agent is allowed to do autonomously. All five screens below represent early-stage concepts for validation before high-fidelity build.

Research objectives

Six primary objectives guide the concept-testing sessions.

RO-1
Mental model alignment

Understand how users conceptualise "headless AI" and whether the proposed UI metaphors (ambient view, policy studio, digital twin) map to their existing mental models of autonomous systems.

Mental model
RO-2
Trust & confidence signals

Assess which UI elements (confidence scores, approval gates, posture radar) build or erode trust in AI-driven automation and whether the signals are legible without explicit instruction.

Trust
RO-3
Oversight & control

Determine whether users feel appropriately in control when the AI operates autonomously and identify the points where they expect — or demand — human approval gates.

Control
RO-4
Transparency of reasoning

Evaluate whether the Decision Reasoning timeline and agentic trace make AI decision logic transparent enough for operators and compliance officers to audit and explain.

Transparency
RO-5
Permission & policy usability

Test whether users can successfully configure autonomy levels in the Policy Studio permission matrix and understand the downstream effects visualised in the posture projection radar.

Policy
RO-6
Pre-flight & digital twin value

Measure the perceived value of the digital-twin simulation environment as a risk-mitigation mechanism before autonomous agent deployment in production environments.

Simulation
03

Key research questions

Organised by objective cluster — each question is tied directly to a task scenario.

Mental model & trust
  1. When participants first see the Canvas dashboard, what story do they construct about what the AI is doing and who is responsible?
  2. Which elements — confidence score, fleet status, escalation cards — do users identify as the primary indicators of system health?
  3. Does displaying an overall 95% confidence score increase or decrease the user's sense of control?
Oversight & escalation
  1. At what point in the agentic trace do users feel compelled to intervene, and what information triggers that compulsion?
  2. How do users interpret the difference between "Auto resolved" and "Escalation" labels in the case list?
  3. Is the concept of an approval gate — a mandatory human checkpoint — intuitive without a tooltip or onboarding step?
Policy & permissions
  1. Can users correctly map the three-mode selector (Auto / Ask / Approval) to their mental model of AI autonomy levels?
  2. After adjusting a permission in the matrix, do users look for and find evidence of how that change affects the posture radar?
  3. What does the composite score (85/100) communicate, and do users trust it as a proxy for compliance readiness?
Digital twin & simulation
  1. Do users understand the digital twin as a safe sandbox, or do they conflate it with the live production environment?
  2. After seeing the SLA compliance test result (Outcome: Contained, 8 min, 5 autonomous acts), do users feel confident deploying to production?
  3. What additional simulation scenarios do users say they would want to run before approving a policy change?

Tasks & discussion guide

Five scenario-based tasks. Each begins with a brief scenario framing, a primary task, and follow-up probes. Screens are presented as static hi-fi prototypes.

Scenario framing

You've just been handed access to a new operations tool your company is piloting. This is the first screen you see. Take a moment to look it over — there's no right or wrong answer.

Primary task

Please think aloud as you explore this screen. Tell me what you think this tool does, who would use it, and what the most important information on the screen is.

Follow-up probes
  • What does "95% confidence" tell you? Is that good or bad?
  • You see an escalation card. What would you do next? Why?
  • Who do you think made the decisions shown here — a person or the system?
  • What's missing that would make you trust this view more?
Screen 01 Mental model Trust
Scenario framing

Your compliance team has asked you to audit the AI's activity over the past week. You're now looking at a list of 40 cases the AI handled.

Primary task

Imagine you need to explain to your manager which cases might require a human review. Walk me through how you'd identify those from this screen.

Follow-up probes
  • What does "Auto resolved" mean to you — confident or risky?
  • You see 82% auto-resolution. Is that a metric you'd track? What's your ideal number?
  • What additional columns or filters would help you do this audit faster?
  • If a case shows "Fix failed" — who is responsible for that outcome?
Screen 02 Oversight Transparency
Scenario framing

A customer has escalated a complaint about case C-2837, saying the AI's action caused a service interruption. You need to understand exactly what happened.

Primary task

Using this screen, reconstruct the sequence of events for case C-2837. At what point — if any — would you have wanted to be alerted?

Follow-up probes
  • Does the confidence trajectory chart help you understand why the AI escalated? What would make it clearer?
  • The approval gate required a human sign-off — did that feel like the right moment? Too early? Too late?
  • How would you use the gate ledger to explain this event to an auditor?
  • Is this level of detail too much, too little, or about right for your role?
Screen 03 Decision reasoning Approval gate
Scenario framing

Your security team wants to allow the AI to automatically patch low-severity vulnerabilities but requires human approval for anything that modifies firewall rules. You need to configure that policy.

Primary task

Using the Policy Studio, set up the permissions to match that requirement. Talk me through your decisions as you go.

Follow-up probes
  • The posture radar changed after you adjusted those permissions — what does that change mean to you?
  • The composite score is 85/100. Does that score give you confidence to proceed, or do you want more information?
  • What would a score of 60/100 tell you? Would you still deploy?
  • What's missing from this screen that you'd expect to see before saving a policy?
Screen 04 Policy Posture radar
Scenario framing

Before rolling out the new policy to your live network, your team wants to run a test in a safe environment. You're now looking at the digital twin simulation panel.

Primary task

A simulation has just run. Review the results and decide whether you would approve this policy for production deployment. Explain your reasoning aloud.

Follow-up probes
  • The result says "Outcome: Contained, 8 min resolution." Is that enough information to approve deployment?
  • The AI took 5 autonomous acts and 1 human intervention — does that ratio feel right for your risk tolerance?
  • What other scenarios would you want to simulate before going live?
  • How does this environment compare to the simulation or sandbox tools you currently use?

Discussion guide outline

60-minute session structure with facilitator timing cues.

0–5 min
Welcome & consent

Introduce the session purpose (without naming the product), confirm recording consent, explain think-aloud protocol, and set expectations for prototype fidelity.

5–12 min
Warm-up — current tools & pain points

Ask about current monitoring and automation tools, key daily workflows, and biggest frustrations with AI or automated systems in their current environment.

12–20 min
Task 1 — Canvas dashboard

Present Screen 01. First impressions, think-aloud exploration. Confidence score, escalation cards, activity timeline probes.

20–30 min
Tasks 2 & 3 — Agentic trace

Present Screens 02 and 03 sequentially. Case list audit task, then C-2837 deep-dive. Decision reasoning timeline and approval gate probes.

30–45 min
Tasks 4 & 5 — Policy Studio & digital twin

Present Screens 04 and 05. Permission matrix configuration task, posture radar exploration, digital twin simulation review and deployment decision.

45–55 min
Concept reaction — overall system

Show all five screens together. Ask for overall system narrative, most valuable screen, biggest concern, and comparison to tools they currently use.

55–60 min
Wrap-up & SEQ ratings

Administer Single Ease Question for Tasks 1 and 4. Collect any final thoughts. Confirm participant incentive and debrief on study purpose.

Risks & mitigations

Anticipated study risks with corresponding mitigation actions and contingency owners.

Risk Likelihood Impact Mitigation Owner
Participant drop-out (< 12 completions) Medium High Over-recruit by 20% (15 screened, 12 confirmed); keep 2 waitlist participants per segment Research coordinator
Prototype fidelity confusion — participants attempt interactions that don't work High Medium Introduce prototype fidelity disclaimer at session start; use Maze click-through hotspots to reduce dead-end interactions Design partner
Social desirability bias — participants over-report trust or competence High Medium Use behavioural tasks (not self-report) as primary metrics; use indirect probes; analyse task performance data independently Lead researcher
Segment imbalance — insufficient compliance officer recruitment Medium Medium Activate secondary recruitment channel (LinkedIn Recruiter) in parallel with UserTesting panel during Week 1 Research coordinator
Technical issues — Zoom or Maze failures during session Low High Have static PDF backup of all five screens; test Maze prototype 48 h before each session batch; keep Teams as backup video platform Research coordinator
Concept too novel — participants unable to engage with agentic AI framing Medium High Add 90-second "what is an AI agent" primer video at session start; adjust screener to exclude participants with zero familiarity with automation tools Lead researcher
