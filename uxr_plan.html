1 Background & context

The IBM Network Intelligence team is exploring a new interaction paradigm for AI-powered network operations: headless AI agents that detect anomalies, make remediation decisions, and take actions autonomously — surfacing to human operators only for escalations, approvals, or audit.

The concept introduces three novel surfaces that operators have never encountered before: a Canvas that shows ambient system confidence rather than alerts, an Agentic Trace that exposes agent decision reasoning, and a Policy & Permissions studio where operators define what an agent is allowed to do autonomously. All five screens below represent early-stage concepts for validation before high-fidelity build.

2 Research objectives

Objective 01 · Comprehension
Do operators understand what the agent did and why?
Evaluate whether the Canvas, Trace, and Decision Reasoning surfaces communicate agent state, actions, and reasoning clearly enough for operators to make confident decisions without guessing.
Objective 02 · Trust calibration
Do operators trust the agent at the right level?
Assess whether confidence scores, approval gates, and posture indicators help operators develop accurate, calibrated trust — neither over-relying nor dismissing agent decisions.
Objective 03 · Decision support
Can operators make good decisions at escalation moments?
Determine whether the escalation card (Canvas) and case detail (Trace) give operators sufficient context to approve, deny, or redirect an agent action within the 26-minute expiry window.
Objective 04 · Policy legibility
Can operators configure and verify agent policy with confidence?
Assess whether the permission matrix, composite score, and digital twin surfaces enable operators to set guardrails, understand the tradeoffs, and verify intended behavior before deployment.
Objective 05 · Mental model fit
Does the "agent as autonomous colleague" model land?
Explore whether operators' existing mental models (alert-driven, human-initiated) conflict with the new ambient/autonomous paradigm — and identify where the biggest conceptual gaps are.
Objective 06 · Anxiety & control
Where do operators feel they have lost control?
Surface moments where the invisible nature of agent operation causes anxiety, confusion, or a desire to intervene — to guide which surfaces need more presence signals or override affordances.

3 Key research questions

Comprehension

  • When looking at the Canvas, what does the operator think the system is doing right now?
  • Does "Confidence Score 95%" communicate health, risk, or something else?
  • Can operators locate which agent handled a specific case and what it decided?
  • Does the Decision Reasoning timeline (Detected → Investigated → Escalated) read as a logical sequence?

Trust

  • At what point does the operator feel comfortable approving an action they didn't initiate?
  • Does seeing a confidence score of 87% make approval easier or harder?
  • What does "82% auto-resolution rate" mean to an operator — is it reassuring or alarming?
  • Does the 4th recurring failure ("CHRONIC TVM-EDGE-04") trigger distrust of the agent?

Decision support

  • What information does the operator need before clicking Approve vs. Escalate?
  • Is the 26-minute expiry window on the escalation card noticed? Does it create pressure?
  • Does "View reasoning trace" get used before deciding? If not, why not?

Policy configuration

  • Does the Auto/Ask/Approval permission model map to how operators think about authorization?
  • What does the Composite Score (85/100) tell the operator — and what would make them change it?
  • Does the Posture Projection radar communicate the autonomy/compliance tradeoff intuitively?
  • Would operators use the digital twin to verify policy before deploying? What are their concerns?

Sessions are moderated and remote. Participants share their screen. Facilitator uses think-aloud protocol throughout. Screens are shown as static images in Figma/Miro — no interactive prototype required.

8 Analysis framework

Coding framework — confusion & trust moments
Confusion: terminology
Participant misunderstands a label, metric, or concept (e.g. "Blast Radius", "Composite Score", "Gate Ledger")
Confusion: missing context
Participant cannot make a decision because they lack information that isn't on screen
Confusion: navigation
Participant cannot find the information they're looking for or takes an unexpected path
Trust: over-reliance
Participant approves without checking evidence or reasoning — no verification behavior
Trust: under-reliance
Participant expresses distrust despite favorable indicators; wants to override agent decisions
Trust: calibrated
Participant checks evidence, reads reasoning, and makes a confident, reasoned decision
Anxiety: loss of control
Participant expresses discomfort that the agent acted without them, or fear of what they might have missed
Mental model: mismatch
Participant maps the new interface onto an old mental model (alert dashboard, ticketing system) in a way that causes errors

10 Risks & mitigations

High Recruitment difficulty Network ops roles are specialized. Recruit 12, expect 10% no-show. Start recruitment in Week 1 day 1. Use LinkedIn, IBM Design research panel, and partner with IBM Network product team for customer contacts.
High Concept too novel to evaluate Participants may not have a reference frame for autonomous agents. Brief them on the premise (2 min) before screens — not enough to bias, but enough to prevent total confusion. Pilot session will validate briefing length.
Medium Static screens limit interaction testing This study tests comprehension and concept resonance — not usability. Frame all tasks as "what would you do / how do you feel" rather than "can you complete this task." Save interaction testing for a prototype study in the next sprint.
Medium Facilitator bias on novel concepts Facilitator must not explain terminology or confirm participant interpretations. Agree on probing language in advance. Use co-facilitator or observer to flag leading questions in debrief.
Low Recording consent issues Send consent form 48h before session. Have verbal and written consent before starting recording. Store recordings in IBM-approved secure storage only.
